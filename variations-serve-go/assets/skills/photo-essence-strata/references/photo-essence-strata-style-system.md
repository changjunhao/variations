# Photo Essence Strata Style System

## Overall aesthetic

One fixed portrait 3:5 archival plate that excavates a single photograph downward through three strata: reality (100%), memory (30%), essence (5%). The plate reads as a page from a quiet museum catalogue of how an image is remembered — photographic fact on top, soft watercolor recollection in the middle, archetypal geometry at the bottom, with a hairline archive line like a specimen label. Solemn, timeless, slightly irregular; never a collage, never a poster, never cute, never an infographic.

The philosophical frame: you are not simplifying an image, you are excavating its archetypal form — biologically returning a bird to its egg, physically reducing entropy to symmetry, philosophically revealing the Platonic ideal, computationally accessing the parent class, mythologically finding the original sketch. The final stratum should feel like a memory from before birth, a blueprint from before construction, a seed from before germination, a symbol from before language.

## Template geometry

- **Canvas** — fixed portrait 3:5 (960×1600). Always portrait, always three strata, regardless of the source photo's orientation.
- **Three strata** — stacked top to bottom: Stratum I (reality) ≈ 28–38% of canvas height, Stratum II (memory) ≈ 28–36%, Stratum III (essence) ≈ 28–36%. Each reads as roughly a third; gentle variation between outputs is allowed for breath, but no stratum may dominate or vanish.
- **Seams** — strata are separated by thin matte warm-ivory seams (narrow quiet gaps, not drawn rules). No borders, frames, rounded corners or cast shadows anywhere.
- **Paper base** — matte warm ivory across Strata II and III, calm and even, at most a barely visible fine paper grain; never yellowed, never grey, never noisy.
- **Fusion variant** — when the scene is extremely spare (a few birds on bare branches, one figure in fog), Strata II and III may dissolve into one continuous excavation field that still reads clearly as a descent from watercolor recollection into archetypal geometry, with the archive line at the very bottom.

## Stratum I — Reality (the photograph)

- A full-width horizontal band of the original photograph at the top of the plate.
- Reproduce the photograph faithfully: same people, scenery, layout, contours, light and native color grading.
- Only crop or reframe into the horizontal band is permitted — choose the most narrative slice (the ducklings' line across the water, the figure leaning on the rail). Landscape sources crop gently; portrait sources reframe to their strongest horizontal band. Never stretch.
- No filter, vignette, border, caption or paper margin on the photo band; it bleeds to the left and right canvas edges.

## Stratum II — Memory (watercolor recollection)

A soft watercolor/gouache recollection of the whole scene on warm ivory — the same place, remembered. It keeps every key element group recognizable (water, birds, figures, vegetation, structures) while letting detail fall away.

**Rendering language:**

- Loose wet-on-wet washes with gentle pigment bleeding; soft dry-brush grain inside larger masses; contours soften and partly dissolve into the paper.
- Flatten photographic depth into layered wash planes; keep the photo's spatial layout readable (horizon stays a horizon, the duckling line stays a dotted procession).
- Delete fine textures, small clutter, cast shadows and photographic light effects; sparkles on water become a few pale flecks or nothing.
- Figures and animals stay recognizable but lose their features — silhouettes and soft masses, faces gone.
- Generous negative space: large areas of untouched ivory are part of the composition.

**Title typography (inside Stratum II):**

- Exactly one title naming the memory: 2–4 words (e.g. "The Small Ones Follow", "Watching the Black Swan", "Yellow Arrives First").
- Fine elegant serif, roman or italic, medium-small relative to the canvas; may be centered, left- or right-aligned, set on one line or broken into short stacked lines — whichever serves the composition.
- Ink color: charcoal, soft dark brown, or a deep tone drawn from the scene; never pure black, never loud.
- Language follows the instruction (default English; 中文 uses a fine serif/song-style face); 无字 removes the title and leaves the paper empty.
- The title is woven into the memory field — it may sit above, below or beside the painted masses, always with quiet paper around it.

## Stratum III — Essence (archetypal reduction)

The archaeological floor of the plate: the scene reduced to its archetypal form. Not a simpler drawing of the scene — the scene's parent class.

**Reduction grammar:**

- Feathers → egg shell: textured bodies become continuous unbroken surfaces (a solid circle, an oval).
- Wings → egg outline: movement becomes the potential for movement — an arc, a crescent, a curve that could take flight.
- Eyes → empty space: watching becomes untouched paper; never draw eyes.
- Beak → asymmetry: direction survives only as a slight off-center placement, a tilt, one shape leaning toward another.

**Mapping primitives:**

- Bodies and heads → circles, ovals, rounded squares, solid dots.
- Horizons, rails, wires, branches → straight bars, one line thick or one band deep.
- Paths, wakes, shorelines, necks → single sweeping arcs or crescents.
- The small and numerous (ducklings, sparks, rain, scattered birds) → a row or drift of small dots.
- Ground, fog, sky → one or two large quiet color fields or broad bands.

**Discipline:**

- 2–4 colors only, all derived from the source photo (its deepest water, its warmest accent, its paper ivory); flat fills with at most a whisper of brush grain — never gradients, never candy gloss.
- Shapes may be slightly irregular, hand-cut, wabi-sabi; but never cartoonish, never outlined stickers, never icon-set neatness.
- Composition: sparse, asymmetric, monumentally calm; large empty areas are structural, not unfinished.
- Pursue archaeological essence, timeless geometry, silent weight, cosmic simplicity. Avoid cute minimalism, decorative simplification, icon design, infographic style.

**Archive line (bottom of Stratum III):**

- One small archival notation row near the bottom edge: left — "NO. 0XX" (a three-digit specimen number); center — the date in DD MMM YYYY uppercase (e.g. "15 AUG 2026"); right — one uppercase theme phrase of 2–3 words naming the essence (e.g. "FOLLOW THE WATER", "THREE STAY", "MORNING HELD").
- Hairline sans-serif, wide letter-spacing, very small; charcoal or a deep scene tone.
- The theme phrase is a distinct, more abstract naming than the Stratum II title — the title remembers the scene, the theme phrase names its essence.
- If 隐藏 is instructed, remove the archive line entirely and leave the paper empty.

## Color discipline

- Every color in all three strata traces back to the source photograph's own tonality.
- Stratum II renders the photo's families lighter, quieter, slightly desaturated; Stratum III keeps only the 2–4 most essential tones at their deepest calm.
- Warm ivory paper is the connective tissue; the photo band supplies the only photographic color.

## Acceptance criteria

The final image contains exactly:

1. a fixed portrait 3:5 plate with three horizontal strata — reality, memory, essence — separated by thin warm-ivory seams;
2. Stratum I: the untouched original photograph, full-width, altered at most by crop or reframe into the band;
3. Stratum II: a soft watercolor recollection of the whole scene on warm ivory — recognizable, flattened, partly forgotten — with exactly one fine serif title woven in;
4. Stratum III: an archetypal flat-geometry reduction in 2–4 scene-derived colors, solemn and non-iconic, with one hairline archive line at the bottom (unless hidden by instruction);
5. no other text, seals, logos, watermarks, frames or ornaments anywhere.

Reject any result with a stylized or recomposed photo band; a realistic repaint or photographic texture in the lower strata; an essence stratum that reads as cute icons, decorative minimalism or an infographic; candy color, gradients or hard sticker outlines; a shouting or misplaced title; a missing or overgrown archive line; strata proportions that collapse the three-part descent; or any element that contradicts the source photograph.
