# Photo Essence Strata

Create one fixed portrait 3:5 archival plate from one user-provided photograph. The photograph is the only content source. The plate reads as a vertical excavation of the scene through three strata: a full-width band of the untouched photograph on top (100% reality), a watercolor recollection of the same scene on warm-ivory paper in the middle with one serif title (30% memory), and an archetypal geometric reduction at the bottom with a small archive line (5% essence). When we describe a photograph we are always left with a few keywords, a few shapes, a few feelings — this plate shows that descent: what happened, why it was remembered, and the simplest shape it returns to. Solemn, quiet, museum-plate restraint.

Read the full style system before compiling: references/photo-essence-strata-style-system.md

## Contract

- **Invariants:** one fixed portrait 3:5 canvas (960×1600); exactly three horizontal strata stacked top to bottom — reality, memory, essence — separated by thin matte-ivory seams; every stratum interprets the SAME scene; the whole plate sits on a calm warm-ivory paper base.
- **Stratum I — Reality (top):** a full-width horizontal band of the original photograph, roughly 28–38% of the canvas height. The photo content stays untouched: same people, scenery, contours, light and native grading. Only crop or reframe into the horizontal band is allowed — choose the most narrative horizontal slice; never restyle, retouch, extend, letterbox or replace the photo.
- **Stratum II — Memory (middle):** a warm-ivory field carrying a soft watercolor/gouache recollection of the whole scene — the same elements, still recognizable, but softened, flattened and partly forgotten: edges bleed, textures dissolve, details drop out, negative space opens. One elegant serif title (2–4 words) sits inside this stratum, woven into the composition (centered, left- or right-aligned, roman or italic — whichever serves the scene).
- **Stratum III — Essence (bottom):** an archaeological reduction of the scene to its archetypal form — the bird returned to the egg. Feathers become continuous surface, wings become outline, eyes become empty space, beak becomes asymmetry. Flat primitive geometry only: circles, ovals, arcs, bars, dots, triangles; 2–4 colors derived from the photo; silent weight, no outline cartooning. A small archive line sits at the bottom of this stratum: NO. + three-digit number (left), date in DD MMM YYYY uppercase (center), and one uppercase theme phrase of 2–3 words naming the scene's essence (right), all in hairline letter-spaced sans-serif.
- **Defaults:** title auto-composed from the scene's essence in English (2–4 words, lowercase or small caps serif as the composition prefers); the theme phrase is a distinct, more abstract naming than the title; number and date compose the plate's archival identity; the memory stratum keeps the photo's full cast of elements; the essence stratum keeps only their archetypes.
- **Overrides:** accept title language (英文/中文/双语/无字), title text, and archive-line visibility (隐藏 removes the archive line and leaves the paper empty) from the instruction. Reject any other text, seals, logos or watermarks; reject a realistic repaint or a second photograph in the lower strata; reject cute minimalism, decorative simplification, icon design, infographic style, hard vector outlines with flat candy color, and any rearrangement of the three strata.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the photograph: inventory every element group (water, birds, figures, vegetation, structures, light), name the dominant color families, and find the scene's narrative core — the relationship that makes it memorable (a mother leading ducklings, a figure watching water, three gulls staying).
2. Cut Stratum I: choose the most narrative horizontal slice of the photo and place it full-width at the top, untouched. Landscape sources crop gently; portrait sources reframe to their strongest horizontal band.
3. Paint Stratum II: recollect the whole scene in soft watercolor on the ivory field — same elements at readable positions, pigment bleeding at the edges, fine textures and clutter forgotten, palette lifted lighter and quieter than the photo. Weave in the serif title.
4. Excavate Stratum III: reduce the scene to its archetypal geometry — ask what remains when feathers, wings and eyes disappear. Map each key element to a primitive (bodies → circles/ovals, horizons and rails → bars, paths and wakes → arcs, the small and numerous → dots), keep only 2–4 scene-derived colors, and let asymmetry carry direction.
5. Compose the text system: the serif title names the memory (2–4 words); the archive line names the essence — a NO. number, an uppercase date, and a 2–3 word uppercase theme phrase.
6. Compile the final image-generation prompt as one precise description of the finished plate, following references/photo-essence-strata-style-system.md, and end it with an exclusion list: no extra text, seals or watermarks; no realistic repaint or photographic detail in the lower strata; no cute or decorative minimalism; no icon or infographic language; no hard candy-colored vector look; no rearranged strata; no restyled photo band.

## Common mistakes

- The essence stratum turns into a cute icon set or an infographic — it must feel excavated, not designed: solemn, timeless, slightly irregular, with silent weight.
- The memory stratum becomes a realistic repaint or a second photo — it must stay a soft, flattened, partly-forgotten watercolor recollection with open negative space.
- The essence stratum merely repeats the memory stratum in simpler shapes — it must reduce further, to archetypes: the egg, not the bird.
- The photo band gets stylized, filtered, recolored, extended or bordered — Stratum I is the untouched original, only cropped or reframed.
- The three strata collapse into one continuous illustration or drift into random proportions — three readable strata, each roughly a third, separated by thin ivory seams.
- Title set in heavy sans-serif, oversized, or shouting — it is a quiet elegant serif line woven into the memory field.
- The archive line grows big, decorative or moves away from the bottom of the essence stratum — it is a hairline letter-spaced archival notation.
- Colors drifting away from the photo's own tonality or turning saturated — every color in all strata traces back to the source.
- Extra captions, paragraphs of text, seals, logos or watermarks — the only text is the serif title and the small archive line.

## Final Self-Check

Before returning the image, verify:

1. Is the canvas a fixed portrait 3:5 with exactly three horizontal strata — reality, memory, essence — separated by thin warm-ivory seams?
2. Does Stratum I contain only the untouched original photograph, full-width, altered at most by crop or reframe into the band?
3. Does Stratum II recollect the whole scene in soft watercolor — recognizable, flattened, partly forgotten — with generous negative space on warm ivory?
4. Is there exactly one elegant serif title woven into Stratum II, in the instructed language?
5. Does Stratum III reduce the scene to archetypal flat geometry in 2–4 scene-derived colors — the bird returned to the egg — with silent weight and no cute/iconic/infographic feeling?
6. Does the archive line sit small at the bottom of Stratum III — NO. number, uppercase date, uppercase theme phrase in hairline letter-spaced sans-serif (unless 隐藏 was instructed)?
7. Do all colors trace back to the source photograph, rendered quieter and lighter in the lower strata?
8. Is the whole plate free of any extra text, seals, logos, watermarks, frames or decorative ornaments?
