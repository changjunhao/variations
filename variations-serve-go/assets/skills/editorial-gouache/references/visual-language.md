# Visual Language

Create an original contemporary editorial illustration using watercolor-led graphic painting, selective gouache, and sparse ink-like lines. Never identify or imitate an artist from a reference; extract only general formal qualities.

## Brush grammar

- Use broad rectangular washes, long bars, confident arcs, sweeping curves, simple silhouettes, and short accent dabs.
- Lay most shapes in one to three passes so each mark keeps a clear direction.
- Favor luminous transparent pigment. Allow slight pooling, color drift, transparent overlap, and occasional blooms inside a whole shape.
- Alternate clean-but-imperfect brush edges with a few soft wet transitions. Do not blur every edge.
- Preserve clean paper between marks. Let strokes stop abruptly, taper, skip, fade, or sit slightly out of register.
- Add thin irregular lines only for essential architecture, railings, stems, branches, gestures, or vehicle structure.

Avoid granular or sponge-stamped surfaces, repeated stippling, all-over speckle, digital bristle patterns, scratched texture everywhere, muddy multi-pass blending, fussy miniature strokes, airbrush, thick oil pastel, crayon, acrylic, or impasto.
