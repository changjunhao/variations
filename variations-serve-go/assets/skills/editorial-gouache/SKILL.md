# Editorial Gouache

Interpret the photograph; do not trace it. Preserve its identity and spatial direction while reducing it to a few memorable shapes.

## Read first

Always read:

- [simplification.md](references/simplification.md) for what to keep and remove;
- [composition.md](references/composition.md) for placement, paper reserve, and irregular edges;
- [visual-language.md](references/visual-language.md) and [color-palette.md](references/color-palette.md) for the painting brief.

Read [examples.md](references/examples.md) only when a scene-type example is useful.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source and identify 1–3 recognition anchors, one structural rhythm, and 3–5 color families.
2. Preserve orientation, travel/facing direction, left-right order, characteristic curves, and essential adjacency. Never mirror unless requested.
3. Remove or merge roughly 50–70% of clutter and micro-detail. Convert what remains into large shapes, repeated marks, and sparse lines.
4. Recompose gently: change crop, spacing, scale, or emphasis, but keep enough source layout that the original is immediately recognizable.
5. Choose one visible emphasis such as oversized flowers, repeated columns, enlarged sky, or an accent-color rhythm.
6. Paint with broad watercolor-led marks, selective gouache accents, and sparse thin linework.

## Required result

- Keep the painted cluster around 70–85% of the sheet by default, centered or flexibly offset according to the subject.
- Leave roughly 15–25% warm paper white around and within the composition.
- Form the outer boundary from independently ending washes and strokes: abrupt stops, tapered tails, dry skips, lost edges, and a few soft bleeds.
- Keep the perimeter asymmetric and irregular. Do not create a rectangle, uniform border, oval vignette, or evenly feathered halo.
- Use 3–5 clear pigment families. Favor luminous transparent washes; reserve opacity and darkest marks for the focal area.
- Build forms with a few decisive bands, bars, arcs, curves, silhouettes, and simple blocks, usually in one to three passes.
- Concentrate recognizable detail in one area; simplify everything else quickly.

## Reject

Reject full-bleed painting, a complete photographic repaint, reversed direction, excessive abstraction that loses the source, artificial white bands, neat frames, decorative watercolor vignettes, fuzzy edges everywhere, granular or sponge texture, stippling, muddy overworking, oil pastel, impasto, airbrush gradients, photorealism, 3D, anime, cartoon outlines, vector-perfect geometry, or imitation of a named artist.

Before delivery, confirm: the source is recognizable at once; the image feels edited rather than traced; the pigment is clear and water-carried; and natural paper white surrounds an open, irregular painted footprint.
