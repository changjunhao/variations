# Visual Reference Grammar (Prose Anchor)

This skill's style family is defined by the prose anchor below, distilled from the approved reference set. The canonical anchor is a river-crossing scene: a stable architectural arch in firm saturated deep-green carved linework on luminous oat-white fibrous paper; one unmistakable small red moving accent riding the arch; pale but still legible blue-gray atmospheric architecture behind a rhythmic foliage band; slow horizontal blue-gray wave dashes below; a two-line tiny typewriter caption set far from the cluster in the quiet upper paper, and a three-line microtext block in a lower corner. Learn the relationships, never the subject: do not reuse the bridge, bus, boats, towers, captions, or layout.

## What the canonical anchor teaches

- The dominant silhouette supplies structure without filling the page (roughly 18%-35%); it may touch a side edge but never floods the page with continuous detail.
- Quiet paper does not weaken the ink: the dark anchor separates decisively from the page, the middle foliage band stays visible, and the small accent stays vivid.
- There is no sticker boundary and no carved frame: buildings and foliage fade into untouched paper; exposed paper sits inside and between forms, not as a uniform outline around the scene.
- Abstraction comes from omission, hierarchy compression, flattened depth (at most three layers), and exposed paper — not from reduced opacity, irregular containers, faithful perspective, binary black/cream contrast, or exhaustive cutting.
- People, vehicles, boats, and trees are compressed into distinct rhythmic mark families rather than documentary detail.

## Variant moods inside the same family

Use only when the atmosphere route demands it; keep the canonical print construction:

- **Warm waterfront memory:** stacked horizontal depth bands, persimmon sunset glow against quiet blue-gray water, small captioning, calm separation between city band and foreground boat. Bands are flat printed ink fields — never torn paper strips, never stickers, unless collage is explicitly requested.
- **Bright playful facade:** repeated architectural units organizing several fresh inks, short warm atmosphere marks, irregular pale contours only around small fragments rather than the whole scene.
- **Dense sunset city:** concentrated orange glow, violet-blue architectural silhouettes, selective window-grid dissolution, strong vertical ascent, tiny type embedded inside the visual cluster; long lines stay structural and inwardly organized, never explosive rays or speed effects.
- **Pale winter shore:** cool blue-white paper, a large quiet field, delicate horizontal water bands, a tiny warm human anchor, low-contrast distance; keep enough blue-gray middle-distance contrast to stay legible at thumbnail; even pale scenes keep visible contour cuts and directional hatch groups, never blue photographic halftone.

## Shared grammar

- Keep the page calm, tall, luminous, and spacious; 35%-55% genuinely untouched paper; dense dark ink below 15%-25% as an area limit, never an opacity limit.
- Paper family: warm cream, pale oat, cool blue-white fibrous paper without stains, burnt edges, or brown kraft heaviness.
- Color limited but alive: two to four flat relief inks plus paper; a full-strength chromatic dark (deep green, mineral blue, blue-gray) over dominant black; a clear value ladder from paper white to firm dark with one small saturated counter-accent when the source supports it.
- Distinguish true empty paper from pale imagery: faintly traced mountains, clouds, or architecture are not negative space.
- Distinguish calm from faded: snow, fog, winter, and solitude may reduce color count but must retain a confident dark anchor and a visible secondary tone.
- Treat exhaustive rendering as drift: reduce logos, hotel names, shop signs, window grids, leaves, and facade ornament to a few anonymous cues.

## Interpretation boundary

Reject seven extremes: a uniformly engraved antique illustration; a flat duotone photograph with no real carved lines; a complete photograph-shaped sticker; a large white-bordered irregular scene blob; a borderless but perspective-faithful full-scene tracing; a traditional black-and-cream linocut with carved border and dense dark perimeter; and a washed-out pale monochrome whose inks merge into the paper. Across all presets, keep relief-cut contours and directional hatching visible but localized, preserve decisive ink strength, redesign framing and scale, compress depth, and rebuild one substantial part of the main form as quiet abstract structure.
