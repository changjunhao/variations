# Photo Ink Echo Production Prompt

Create one two-part editorial composition from one user-provided photograph. Use a strict 1:1 split whose arrangement follows the source orientation: for a landscape or near-square photo, the photographic region on top and the panel below; for a portrait photo, the photographic region on the left and the panel to its right. The two regions are equal in size, so the canvas is exactly the photograph doubled along the joining axis.

- Highest-Priority Visual Reference
- Photographic Region: Original Photograph

## Highest-Priority Visual Reference

Inspect every image in assets/examples before generating.

Treat those examples as the highest-priority reference for watercolor hierarchy, lightness, delicate incompleteness, motif scale, and surrounding silence.

Use the examples only as style evidence. Do not copy their subjects, layouts, palettes, or titles. The uploaded photograph remains the only content source.

## Photographic Region: Original Photograph

Use the original uploaded photograph in the photographic region. For a landscape or near-square source, the photographic region sits on top with the panel below; for a portrait source, it sits on the left with the panel to its right. Never choose the other arrangement for the given orientation.

Allow only crop or reframe.

Do not repaint, stylize, reconstruct, retouch, extend, replace, or otherwise change any photographic content. Do not add, remove, or redraw people, objects, architecture, light, or environment.

## Memory Selection Logic

Do not ask:

> What is everything in this photograph?

Ask:

> What is the one thing the eye remembers first?

> What are the minimum secondary cues needed to preserve the memory of the scene?

Select exactly one unmistakable primary subject.

Select only two to four supporting cues. A supporting cue may be one horizon, reflection, gesture, color wash, shadow, shoreline, mast rhythm, or nearby object relationship.

Keep the total information budget to approximately three to five visual relationships. Delete everything else.

A tightly connected group may count as one primary subject only when the photograph clearly reads it as one unit. Do not use this exception to retain a crowd.

## Panel: Very Small Watercolor Memory Motif

Use a clean warm-ivory or soft off-white panel, equal in size to the photographic region, with only a barely visible fine-art watercolor-paper texture.

Do not shrink and repaint the original photograph. Do not create a second complete illustration.

Create only a very small watercolor memory motif containing:

- one clear primary subject
- two to four quiet supporting traces
- large areas of silence around them

Paint the primary subject first. Make it the clearest, most recognizable, and locally highest-contrast part of the motif.

Render supporting cues noticeably lighter, paler, softer, less detailed, and less complete.

Reduce background context to extremely faint color, wash, partial contour, reflection, or disappearing-edge evidence. Do not preserve a complete environment.

## Hierarchy

- Keep the primary subject readable at a glance.
- Allow the primary subject to carry the only restrained dark or saturated accent.
- Reduce opacity, contrast, edge completeness, and detail for every supporting cue.
- Never give multiple elements equal visual weight.
- Never let supporting cues compete with the primary subject.
- Never let the background become a complete scene.

## Correct Meaning of Fragmented

Interpret fragmented as local interruption and selective absence, not blur, disorder, or weak subject recognition.

Correct:

- partially broken contours
- secondary edges disappearing into paper
- controlled gaps
- unfinished pale layers
- a few fragments clustered near the subject

Incorrect:

- an unclear primary subject
- random dots
- decorative splatter
- unrelated scattered objects
- visual noise
- reduced recognition merely to appear fragmented

Keep the primary subject clear.

## Motif Scale and Negative Space

Keep the watercolor motif very small. Treat the examples as the maximum acceptable information density, not as permission to enlarge or complete the scene.

Aim for a compact envelope of approximately 25–38% of the panel width and approximately 16–28% of the panel height. Keep actual painted coverage even sparser.

Leave approximately 82–90% of the panel visually silent.

Place the motif-and-title unit near the visual center of the panel. Do not enlarge it to fill available space.

When in doubt, make the motif smaller and remove more information.

## Watercolor Language

Emphasize:

- light
- fragmented
- pale
- airy
- delicate
- unfinished
- transparent watercolor
- soft disappearing edges
- large negative space
- quiet editorial composition

Use transparent watercolor, diluted pigment, soft bleeding, occasional light ink, disappearing edges, incomplete color layers, and minimal dry-watercolor texture.

Use only colors traceable to the source photograph. Select approximately two to four color families. Keep most color low-saturation, low-contrast, and highly transparent.

## Strict Prohibitions

Do not create:

- full-scene watercolor rendering
- miniature complete painting
- large watercolor motif
- too many objects
- equal visual importance between elements
- dense environment detail
- heavy rendering
- hard borders
- rectangular illustration blocks
- oval vignette
- poster-like composition
- realistic scene reconstruction
- thick or opaque paint masses
- harsh black outlines
- decorative splatter or scattered filler
- auxiliary lines, perspective guides, grids, or annotations
- cartoon treatment

Do not place the motif inside a rectangle, oval wash, framed panel, badge, complete background shape, or poster-style graphic container.

## Typography

Place one original poetic English title directly below the motif. Use two to five words.

Set it in a very small, very thin, modern minimalist sans-serif face with slightly increased letter spacing.

Use charcoal, muted dark brown, or another restrained source-derived dark color.

Do not generate any other text, random characters, subtitles, labels, captions, signatures, or watermarks.

## Final Deletion Check

Before returning the result, verify:

1. Does the photographic region contain only the original photograph, changed solely by crop or reframe, in the arrangement dictated by the source orientation?
2. Is there exactly one clear primary subject on the panel?
3. Are there only two to four supporting cues?
4. Is the total information budget approximately three to five visual relationships?
5. Are supporting cues visibly lighter, paler, and less complete than the primary subject?
6. Has the environment been reduced to faint traces?
7. Does the primary subject remain clear despite controlled fragmentation?
8. Is the motif very small and surrounded by warm-ivory silence?
9. Does the panel avoid every complete-scene, block, vignette, and poster-like treatment?

If it looks like a small complete watercolor illustration, it contains too much information. Delete, fade, and reduce it again.

If it looks like a clear but incomplete remembered fragment, it is correct.

Return only the final composition.
