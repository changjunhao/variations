# Photo Ink Study Style System

## Overall aesthetic

A matte warm-ivory album postcard from an eastern-minimal architecture-and-landscape study: the real photograph in one region, its flat ink-wash deconstruction in the adjoining equal-size region, one hairline serif title at the bottom of the panel. The piece reads as one quiet printed page — generous negative space, restrained palette, soft even presence — never a collage, a poster, or a souvenir card.

## Template geometry

A strict 1:1 two-part composition that follows the source orientation — the canvas is exactly the photograph doubled along the joining axis:

1. **Arrangement** — landscape or near-square source: stacked, photo region on top, panel below; portrait source: side-by-side, photo region on the left, panel to its right. Never the other arrangement for a given orientation. The two regions are exactly equal in size.
2. **Photo region** — the original photograph filling its region completely at its native aspect. Never stretch, never restyle, no letterboxing.
3. **Ink-study panel** — the adjoining equal-size region on matte warm-ivory paper with at most a barely visible fine paper texture; flat and calm, never yellowed, never grey, never noisy. The deconstruction sits inside the panel with generous breathing room — either a soft contained wash block with organic bleeding edges (suited to night and dense city scenes) or an open floating composition dissolving into the paper (suited to bright, pale scenes). Never a hard rectangular frame.
4. **Title (panel bottom)** — one line centered near the bottom of the panel with quiet paper around it; in the stacked arrangement this places it near the canvas bottom, in the side-by-side arrangement near the panel's lower edge.

Region sizes, the equal split, and the title's panel-bottom position never drift between outputs.

## Photo region

- Reproduce the original photograph faithfully: same people, scenery, layout, contours, light and native low-saturation color grading, filling its region completely at native aspect.
- Only crop or reframe is permitted, and only when the composition clearly benefits; prefer the native frame.
- No letterboxing, no paper flanks, no added filter, vignette, border, shadow or caption on the photo.

## Ink-study panel: whole-scene deconstruction

The panel is a flat ink-wash deconstruction of the ENTIRE scene — a study of the same place, not a single motif and not a repaint.

**Extraction and restaging:**

- Inventory every element group of the photo: sky, skyline masses, landmark structures, water, reflections, vegetation, boats, bridges, ridges, figures, foreground.
- Map each group to its simplest geometric mass — towers and buildings become plain rectangular or stepped volumes, roofs become clean angled bands, arches become single sweeping bands, mountains become layered triangular washes, tree lines become rounded dab masses, boats become minimal hull-and-sail shapes.
- Restage every mass at its original relative position and scale relationship, so the flattened scene stays instantly recognizable as the same place.
- Water reflections become simplified mirrored wash blocks directly beneath their sources; they may be softer and more broken than what they mirror.

**Rendering language:**

- Layered soft color blocks in light brush-ink wash; gentle pigment bleeding at edges; subtle dry-brush grain inside larger masses.
- No sharp hard lines anywhere; fine ink contour lines appear only sparingly where recognition requires them (a statue's robe folds, a boat hull, bare branches, cable stays).
- Delete all fine textures, small clutter, cast shadows, and photographic light effects; night glow becomes flat warm color masses, not light bloom.
- Crowds and small figures shrink to one or two tiny dark silhouettes, or disappear entirely.

**Color:**

- Every color family traces back to the source photo's own tonality — night scenes keep deep blue-green ink washes with warm amber architecture; bright day scenes stay pale misty blue-grey; autumn scenes keep their gold.
- Render each family flatter and slightly lighter than the photo; low-contrast neighbors merge into one wash.
- Keep the palette restrained: roughly 3–5 source-derived color families plus ink grey.

## Title typography

- Exactly one line of text on the whole canvas: 2–4 words naming the scene's essence (e.g. RIVER CITY NIGHT, SNOW MOUNTAIN ROAD), all caps.
- Position: centered near the bottom of the panel — in the stacked arrangement this is near the canvas bottom; in the side-by-side arrangement it sits near the panel's lower edge.
- Fine hairline elegant serif, wide letter-spacing, small relative to the canvas — quiet, not a headline.
- Ink color: charcoal, soft dark brown, or a deep tone drawn from the scene; never pure black, never colored loudly.
- Language follows the instruction (default English; 中文 titles use a fine serif/song-style face); 无字 removes the title and leaves the paper empty.
- No subtitle, date, seal, rule, ornament, logo or watermark anywhere.

## Acceptance criteria

The final image contains exactly:

1. a strict 1:1 two-part composition following the source orientation — stacked for landscape or near-square, side-by-side for portrait;
2. the original photograph filling its region completely, untouched in content, composition and grading;
3. one flat ink-wash deconstruction of the whole scene on the adjoining beige panel — every element group geometrically simplified, layered, soft-edged, in source-derived colors, with all fine texture, clutter, shadow and light effects deleted;
4. one hairline serif all-caps title centered near the bottom of the panel.

Reject any result with a stylized, recomposed, letterboxed or shrunken photo region; a realistic repaint or photographic texture in the panel; a panel reduced to one small motif; hard vector outlines; colors that drift from the source tonality or turn saturated; a sans-serif, oversized or misplaced title; extra text, seals, rules, frames or watermarks; unequal region sizes; or an arrangement that contradicts the source orientation.
