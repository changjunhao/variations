# Photo Ink Study

Create one matte warm-ivory album postcard from one user-provided photograph. The photograph is the only content source. The layout follows the source orientation with a strict 1:1 split: for a landscape or near-square photo, the photo on top with the beige panel below; for a portrait photo, the photo on the left with the panel to its right. The two regions are equal in size, so the canvas is exactly the photograph doubled along the joining axis. One region holds the untouched original photograph; the adjoining beige panel holds a flat ink-wash deconstruction of the whole scene, and one hairline serif title sits at the bottom of the panel. Quiet eastern editorial restraint, generous negative space.

Read the full style system before compiling: references/photo-ink-study-style-system.md

## Contract

- **Invariants:** strict 1:1 equal split that follows the source orientation — stacked for landscape or near-square sources, side-by-side for portrait sources, never the other arrangement for a given orientation; one region holds the original photograph, the adjoining equal-size beige panel holds one flat ink-wash deconstruction covering every element of the scene; one fine serif title at the bottom of the panel; the panel sits on a matte warm-ivory paper base.
- **Photo region:** the photo fills its region completely at its native aspect — the region IS the photograph, so no letterboxing and no layout-driven cropping are ever needed. Only crop or reframe is allowed, and only when the composition clearly benefits; never repaint, stylize, reconstruct, retouch, extend or replace the photo's content, people, light or color grading.
- **Whole-scene deconstruction:** the panel must deconstruct the ENTIRE scene, not a single motif. Every element group — sky, skyline masses, landmark structures, water, reflections, vegetation, boats, bridges, ridges, figures — is extracted, geometrically simplified and restaged at its original relative position, so the panel instantly reads as the same place, flattened.
- **Defaults:** the title is auto-composed from the scene's essence (2–4 words, all caps, English unless instructed otherwise); the ink palette strictly follows the source photo's own color tonality, rendered flatter and slightly lighter; the painted field may be a soft contained wash block or an open floating composition, whichever suits the scene.
- **Overrides:** accept title language (英文/中文/双语/无字) and title text from the instruction. Reject any extra text, seals, ornaments, logos or watermarks; reject a realistic repaint or a second photograph in the panel; reject hard vector outlines, oversaturated color, unequal region sizes, and any arrangement that contradicts the source orientation.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the photograph: inventory every visible element group (sky, skyline masses, landmark structures, water, reflections, vegetation, boats, figures, foreground), name the dominant color families and their tonal relationships, and note the source orientation.
2. Fix the layout from the source orientation: landscape or near-square → photo on top, panel below; portrait → photo on the left, panel to its right. Place the photo so it fills its region completely. Preserve its content, composition and native grading exactly; at most crop or reframe.
3. Deconstruct the whole scene onto the beige panel: map every element group to its simplest geometric mass at its original relative position, keeping the photo's spatial layout readable.
4. Render each mass as layered soft color blocks with light brush-ink wash and gentle bleeding edges; soften every contour; delete all fine textures, small clutter, cast shadows and photographic light effects. Reserve a few fine ink contours only where recognition requires them (a statue's folds, a boat hull, bare branches).
5. Preserve signature spatial relationships: water reflections become simplified mirrored wash blocks, bridge arches stay clean single bands, tree lines become rounded dab masses, crowds shrink to one or two small dark silhouettes or disappear.
6. Compose the title: 2–4 words naming the scene's essence, all caps, hairline elegant serif, wide letter-spacing, charcoal or soft dark-brown ink, centered at the bottom of the panel with quiet paper around it.
7. Compile the final image-generation prompt as one precise description of the finished postcard, following references/photo-ink-study-style-system.md, and end it with an exclusion list: no extra text, seals or watermarks; no realistic repaint or photographic detail in the lower zone; no hard crisp outlines; no oversaturated or off-palette color; no restyled or recomposed photo region; no unequal regions; no arrangement that contradicts the source orientation.

## Common mistakes

- The panel becomes a realistic repaint or a second photo — it must stay flat, geometric and washed, with photographic texture fully deleted.
- The panel shrinks to one small motif — that is a different style; here the whole scene must be deconstructed and restaged at comparable visual weight to the photo.
- Hard vector outlines or crisp edges — contours are soft wash boundaries; fine ink lines appear only sparingly for recognition.
- Palette drifting away from the source — every color family in the deconstruction must trace back to the photo's own tonality, only flatter and lighter.
- The photo region getting stylized, recolored, extended or recomposed — the upper zone is the untouched original.
- Portrait sources forced into a stacked layout or letterboxed — for a portrait source the photo goes on the left and the panel on the right, each filling its half.
- Title set in sans-serif, oversized, bold, or placed away from the panel — it is a small hairline serif line at the bottom of the panel.
- Extra text, dates, seals, decorative rules, frames or watermarks — the title is the only text.
- Regions drifting away from the strict 1:1 equal split, or the arrangement contradicting the source orientation (stacked is only for landscape or near-square sources; side-by-side only for portrait sources).

## Final Self-Check

Before returning the image, verify:

1. Does the layout follow the source orientation with a strict 1:1 split — stacked for landscape or near-square, side-by-side for portrait?
2. Does the photo region contain only the original photograph, filling its region completely, altered at most by crop or reframe?
3. Does the panel deconstruct every element group of the scene at its original relative position?
4. Are all shapes geometric, simplified, layered soft color blocks with light ink-wash bleeding and no sharp hard lines?
5. Does every color trace back to the source photo's tonality, rendered flatter and lighter?
6. Have fine textures, clutter, cast shadows and photographic light effects fully disappeared from the panel?
7. Is there exactly one hairline serif title in all caps with wide letter-spacing at the bottom of the panel, and no other text or ornament anywhere?
8. Is the matte warm-ivory paper calm, with generous negative space and at most a barely visible fine paper texture?
